# Storefront Visual Review — Local Browser Captures

**Reviewed:** 2 November 2026  
**Routes supplied:** Home, Shop, Story, Contact  
**Theme:** Desert Atelier — Contemporary Heritage

This review is based on the supplied local browser screenshots. It does not claim to cover unprovided routes or viewport sizes.

## Overall assessment

The visual direction is coherent and recognisable: warm ivory, charcoal/ink, terracotta details, Cormorant Garamond display typography and restrained page composition. The homepage has a stronger identity than a generic catalogue template.

The application is not yet production-ready as a commerce storefront. The most material limitation visible in the captures is the lack of original gallery photography: the same textile-pattern placeholder appears in the hero, gallery introduction, every collection card and all four development product cards. The pattern is a useful visual motif, but it cannot substitute for item photography on a product-selling website.

## Findings by route

### Homepage

**What works**
- Clear brand positioning and editorial headline.
- Strong contrast between the hero, gallery introduction, collections, featured catalogue and story section.
- Collection and product cards are visually consistent.
- The page ends with a direct enquiry action and a distinct dark footer.

**Needs improvement**
- Replace the repeated placeholder with owner-approved hero, collection and product images. Do not reuse one image for unrelated products.
- Keep product-specific image alt text and image rights evidence connected to each product.
- Ensure the hero has a dedicated approved campaign/brand image or a deliberately designed non-product graphic.
- Review the small uppercase metadata at actual device size; it should remain secondary but readable.
- Add real product information and prices only after merchant verification. Preview records must not be mistaken for purchasable inventory.

### Shop

**What works**
- The page hierarchy is easy to scan.
- Search, category and sort controls are visible before the product grid.
- Cards provide a consistent path to product details.

**Needs improvement**
- Four demo products all use the same placeholder, which weakens product comparison and buyer confidence.
- A launch catalogue should display actual product photography, price/price-on-enquiry state, availability and accurate product attributes.
- Review filter behaviour with a larger approved catalogue; the supplied capture shows only four preview records.
- Confirm card links, search state, category filter, sorting and empty-result behaviour on mobile and desktop.

### Story

**What works**
- The page title and gallery context are clear.
- The text makes careful statements about verification and representation of makers.

**Needs improvement**
- The previous page was largely a wide placeholder image followed by a narrow text column, leaving the editorial narrative visually underdeveloped.
- Use a stronger editorial layout with a clear text measure, a supporting location/identity panel and a direct shop/contact close.
- Label the textile pattern as a design placeholder until it is replaced with an approved image.

### Contact

**What works**
- The email address is explicit.
- The page does not invent delivery charges, service promises or shipping times.
- It advises customers to confirm item-specific details.

**Needs improvement**
- The previous text-only presentation did not create a strong enquiry action.
- Give the email a prominent, obvious action treatment.
- Explain which information helps the gallery respond: product name/link, destination and the question.
- Do not invent a phone number, address, WhatsApp account or opening hours. Add those only after owner confirmation.

## Commerce readiness gaps

These screenshots do not demonstrate a complete retail journey. The next acceptance review must also cover product detail, cart drawer, cart page, checkout, login/account, wishlist and order confirmation.

Before launch, verify:
- Actual product images and approved image rights.
- Real product prices, currencies, stock and variants.
- Delivery and returns information supplied by the business.
- End-to-end order creation and payment-state integrity.
- Responsive behaviour, keyboard navigation and contrast.
- No development-only placeholder record appears as real production inventory.

## Owner portal acceptance

The owner should be able to upload an approved image, attach it to a product, add descriptive alt text, record usage-rights evidence, choose a primary image and control its display order. Publishing should remain blocked until required product details and image permissions are satisfied.
